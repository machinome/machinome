## Why

The Enigma I reconstruction (`projects/enigma-i`, root class `simulation/machine.py:Enigma`, a clocked machine with the drivers `case_open`, `hood_open`, `flap_open`, `supply_connected`, `rotors_out` and the `operation.*` inputs, and the instructions `Key A` to `Key Z`, `Press`, `Release`, the wheel moves and the case, hood, flap and rotor moves) is operated today through the panel the viewer builds from those declarations: a side rail of dozens of handles, readouts and buttons (`viewer.ts` `rebuildClockedChrome`, 1886-2058). A reader cannot type on it. The pilot wants the model to carry its own **app**, a React application kept in the project's source beside the model, which replaces that automatic panel wherever the viewer shows the model: for the Enigma, an on-screen keyboard in the machine's layout along the bottom (click Q and the key is pressed, the other 25 are disabled and that key reads "Release Q"), a box of case controls, a box of rotor controls with the three window numbers, and the picture filling the rest.

Two things in the viewer stand in the way. A page cannot be told when a clocked machine's committed bank changes or when a drawn transition finishes: `MachineHandle` (`viewer.ts` 222-229, built at 2686-2742) is the pure `ClockedMachine` plus the clock's two verbs and carries no subscription, while the running handle has `onCommit`/`onOutcome` (199-200) and the posed table `onDriverChange` (319). The keyboard needs both facts, whoever moved the machine. And nothing lets a model's own code take the panel's place: the panels are built straight into the container (`rebuildDriverChrome` 2205, `rebuildRunChrome` 2274, `rebuildClockedChrome` 1886) and the canvas is the container's child, sized by an observer on the container (438, 2373-2396).

## What Changes

- A document may name its model's **app**: a script, with an optional stylesheet, beside the document (the framework's companion change `declare-a-models-app` publishes it), or the sentence saying why the app could not be built. Where it names a script, the viewer's `mount()` loads it and the app takes the place of every automatic panel (posed, running and clocked), of the instruction buttons and of the animation bar. Because the export page, the `machinome develop` page and the Studio all mount through `mount()`, all three show the app with no change of their own. A document naming no app is mounted exactly as today.
- The app owns the layout: it fills the area the host gave the viewer and places the **picture** (the canvas and the corner full-screen control) where it renders `<Viewport/>`; the picture follows the size of the place it is put in. The inspector's sidebar and its navigator stay beside the app, as they stand beside the panel today.
- The contract between the viewer and an app is **framework-neutral**: a classic script that, when it runs, hands the viewer one start function; the viewer calls it with the element to fill, the picture's element, the mount's handle and a way to refuse, and keeps the disposer it returns. **The viewer's bundle stays free of React.** React is the convention of a small helper, `@machinome/viewer/app`: `<Viewport/>`, `useMachine()` (the committed bank and whether a transition is being drawn), `usePlay()` (play a declared instruction) and `useViewer()` (the handle).
- The viewer **bundles** a model's app with its own toolchain: the provider of the web viewer gains the seam operation `bundle`, which the framework asks through its seam. It bundles the app's `.tsx`/`.ts`/`.jsx`/`.js` and `.css` with the viewer's own esbuild, its own copy of React and React DOM and the helper into one minified script and at most one stylesheet, content-named, carrying React's MIT notice. A project carries only its sources, no `package.json`. An import of anything but the app's own files, React, React DOM and the helper is refused by name, and so is a file outside the project.
- **A limit of this change, not a contract:** an app is bundled where the viewer's JavaScript dependencies are installed, which is a source checkout of the repository such as this workspace. An installed `machinome-viewer` (a wheel) carries no app toolchain and refuses, naming that limit; a checkout whose dependencies are not installed refuses naming `npm ci` at its workspace root. Nothing installs a dependency. Putting the toolchain into an installed copy is a later step this change does not take and does not shape.
- The clocked machine gains the smallest notification the app needs: `ViewerHandle.onMachineChange(listener)`, called once after every accepted operation that changes the committed bank or starts or ends a drawn transition, whoever made it (a panel, a host, the clock's transport, a dragged slider, a republish), and `MachineHandle.drawing()`, whether a drawn transition is running now. It survives a republish that replaces the machine, which a subscription on a `MachineHandle` would not (each `machine()` call wraps the machine it found).
- **Refused by name, never substituted:** a document's app refusal, a script that fails to load, a script that starts no app, a start or a render that throws, an app that places no `<Viewport/>`, and the helper's hooks on a model that is not clocked each show the model filling the area with a notice naming the reason in the app's place, with no automatic panel and with the mount resolving. A malformed `app` record refuses the document by name, as a malformed `markings` list does.
- The app is not loaded where the automatic panel is not presented: under `driverControls: 'none'`, which every on-demand mount (`machinome snapshot --viewer web` and a movie's take) already requires (`options.ts` 146; `capture.py` 144, 179; the movie's `stage/runtime.tsx` 288). A republished document keeps a running app whose script and stylesheet are unchanged and replaces one that changed, with the picture standing in between. `dispose()` unmounts the app before anything it reads is released.
- Viewer API rises from 30 to **31**; the document versions read stay 1 to 14. The `app` record is additive: an older viewer ignores it and shows the automatic panel, which is a truthful, operable rendering of the declared machine.

## Capabilities

### New Capabilities

- `model-app`: a model's own app shown in the viewer's place of its automatic panel; its layout and picture; the helper; the refusals; republish and disposal; the bundling of an app by the viewer's toolchain; the API 31 gate.

### Modified Capabilities

- `viewer-package`: "The host chooses how driver controls are presented" is restated so that the one switch also gates a model's app, and the automatic panels are presented by default only where no app is shown (every baseline scenario kept, two added); "A maker watches the model full screen" is restated so that, while an app is shown, the corner control stands in the picture's corner (every baseline scenario kept, one added); a requirement is added for the clocked machine's change notification and `drawing()`.
- `viewer-distribution`: "Distributions carry the built frontends" is restated so that a wheel carries no app toolchain and an export's "exactly two files" are the viewer's two beside the app's own (every baseline scenario kept); a requirement is added saying where an app can be bundled (a source checkout with the viewer's dependencies installed) and what an installed copy answers.
- `user-documentation`: a requirement is added for the manual's pages on a model's app and the new handle members.

Review, 22 November 2026. The reviewer reviewed this change and its companion `declare-a-models-app` adversarially under the pilot's standing delegation and accepted both with one blocking finding: `develop` following the app stopped at "the document is republished" and rested on an inferred reload timing, and an app edit restarted the builder, so the next one loaded the whole model again before it could bundle. Resolution: the timing was observed on the bench (the companion's `evidence/develop-reload-observation.md`: the page is told before the build publishes and shows each edit one edit late); the framework now tells the pages when the document is published and republishes an app edit from the builder that holds the model (companion design Decision 5); this change's design Decision 9 states how the development page then calls `manifestChanged()` and swaps the app with the picture standing, the `model-app` delta carries the scenario "The open development page shows the edited app, the picture standing", and the Chromium test B6 proves it on this side (the companion's X2 end to end). The reviewer's rulings are recorded in design "Rulings": the inspector's rail and sidebar stay beside an app; `useMachine()` stands; no type check of an app's sources. One question stays open for the pilot, the helper's licence (design "Open Questions"); it does not block. This is agent review under delegation, not a human review of the artifacts, and authorizes no integration, push or publication.
