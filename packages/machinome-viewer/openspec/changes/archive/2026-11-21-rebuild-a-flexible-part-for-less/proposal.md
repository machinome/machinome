## Why

On the Enigma I page (`enigma-i` at `081f4e6`, viewer at framework `cb0a5958`), a frame of a key press is 111 ms of script, and two parts of it are the viewer's own work around molejo's evaluator. `FlexibleShape.evaluate` reads each parameter with `evalExpr`, which looks the expression's text up and opens a scope of the expression store for it alone: 36,415 reads a frame over the 86 parts the press reaches, 16 to 17 ms, to rebuild six or seven. After a rebuild it calls three.js's `computeBoundingBox` and `computeBoundingSphere`, which pass over the position attribute three times through its accessors: about a million vertices a frame, 16 to 19 ms. Two prototypes brought these to about 10 ms and about 5 ms, with no difference from the shipped path in 2.1 million parameter values and 349 rebuilds' bounds compared on that page (`workflow/warts.md`, "A flexible part reads every parameter by its text and walks its vertices three times for its bounds"; `workflow/evidence/enigma-flexible-rebuild-cost-2026-11-21.md`). The page has only finite, non-empty parts, so that comparison does not settle the cases it did not meet; this change settles them before it takes the time.

## What Changes

- A flexible part keeps the root node of each of its parameter expressions together with the expression store's generation they were prepared in. A read of its parameters opens one scope of the store, checks the generation inside it, and evaluates each parameter by its root, in the order of their names. When the generation moved, when the part was rebound, or on its first read, each text is prepared again just before its own evaluation, so every refusal is the one `evalExpr` would make, of the same parameter, at the same point. `evaluate` and `admit` use this one reader.
- The conversion of a value to a number moves into one function of `evaluator.ts` that `evalExpr` and the reader both call, so the two cannot convert differently.
- A rebuilt part's bounding box and sphere are computed by one function of `flexible.ts`: one loop over the position array for the box, one for the sphere about the box's centre, with the operations three.js 0.156.1 performs, in its order, so the ten numbers are the same under `Object.is`. It writes into the geometry's existing `Box3` and `Sphere` when there are any, as three.js does. It runs only on a plain three-component `Float32Array` attribute read through three.js's own accessors with no morph targets; on anything else it calls three.js's two functions. When a bound comes out not a number it calls three.js's two functions afterwards, so the console reports exactly what three.js reports.
- Tests that are red on the base by count: text lookups (`prepare` calls) and scopes opened (`withExpressions` calls) during a warm read, for `evaluate` and for `admit`, and calls of the position attribute's accessors during a rebuild. Parity proofs against `evalExpr` (values and refusals, a reclamation between two reads included) and against three.js's own functions (edge arrays and arrays molejo produces, console reports included).
- One existing test asserts that a rebuild calls `computeBoundingBox` and `computeBoundingSphere` once each; it is changed to assert the bounds were written and equal three.js's. Four tests that assert those functions are not called on a kept or judged part gain a check that no bounds were written at all, since the spies alone would now pass whether or not a pass ran.
- Unchanged: which parts are visited and how many reads a frame makes, when a part is rebuilt, molejo and how it is bundled, `expressions.ts`, the flat pass, the renderer, and everything a part draws. Nothing lowers a part's resolution or sampling.

## Capabilities

### New Capabilities

None.

### Modified Capabilities

- `viewer-package`: "The viewer evaluates flexible geometry per frame" states what was implicit while the viewer called `evalExpr` and three.js for these: a part's parameter values and refusals are those of evaluating each expression by itself, in the order of their names, whether the pose is shown or judged; a part prepares its expressions only on its first read, after they or the bindings were replaced, and after the store was reclaimed; and a rebuilt part's bounds are, number for number, the ones three.js computes from its vertex data. Four scenarios are added; the six existing ones are restated unchanged.
