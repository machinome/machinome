## Why

On the Enigma I page (`enigma-i` at `081f4e6`, viewer at framework `f6119628`) the pilot drags the slider that opens the case: the case stands still for as long as the thumb is held, and at the release the thumb goes back to where the input stood and the case swings to the new value in a fifth of a second. The clocked panel's slider listens on `change` alone (`viewer.ts` 4090), so a whole drag is one request made at the release and drawn from the origin. A real pointer measured it: twelve `input` events and no mesh moved, then nine frames of drawing after the release (`workflow/evidence/enigma-dragged-slider-2026-11-22.md`; `workflow/warts.md`, "A clocked machine's slider moves nothing until it is released"). This is not a regression: the clocked slider has listened on `change` alone since it was written on 17 September 2026, and ADR-065 states "there is no live drag". The pilot asks for the case to follow the thumb. The first requirement of this change is that the machine, the model and the panel agree at every moment a maker can see; the second is that the drag is smooth.

## What Changes

- A press on a clocked input's slider, held and moved, is a **drag**. Each position the thumb reaches is **followed**: one request to that position, posed at once and not drawn, made in the next animation frame for the latest position, so the model, the row's reading and the readouts of the states its commits write show the machine's answer within the frame, while the thumb stays under the pointer. At most one request a frame; a thumb held still asks nothing and draws nothing; the last position is never left unasked; no position is asked twice in succession.
- Which change of a slider is followed and which is drawn is decided by the order of the browser's events, not by where it paints the thumb: the first position of a press reported before the press's pointer has moved (a press on the track, where the browser moves the thumb to the press point) is a jump and is drawn as one request, as a track click is today; so is the first position of a press on an input that stood outside its range (a pinned thumb). The positions after such a jump wait for its drawing to land. Every other position of a press is followed. A change outside a press (an arrow key, Page Up and Down, Home and End, a held key, a `change` a script dispatches) stays one request drawn over its duration, as ratified (ADR-065, ADR-073). Measured for this proposal in headless Chromium (mouse and touch) and Firefox: design Decision 1.
- The panel is not rebuilt while a press lasts: the slider the pointer holds stays the element it holds, and keeps its focus after the release. Each request of a drag writes the panel through narrow writers: the row's field, the readouts its commits moved, and the row's outcome line, which reports the drag as a whole (the travel admitted since the press, and the stops or the refusal of its latest position) and is written only when its text changes. The held thumb is never written; when the press is over it is put where the machine stands, pinned at an end if that is outside the range.
- A press ends at the pointer's release, its cancellation or a lost capture; no `change` event is needed. The window losing focus or the page being hidden ends nothing: a thumb the hand still holds goes on being followed. Anything that would move or repose the machine or replace the panel while a press lasts (a typed value, a nudge, an instruction, the host's `machine().move`, `restore`, `reset`, `trigger`, `setClockPlaying`, a change of focus, a reload, an in-place update) ends the press first, its last position asked before it acts; a republish that replaces the machine, and `dispose()`, end it with nothing more asked.
- While the clock's transport plays, where the machine declares a clock, no press begins and a slider is as it is today. The transport rebuilds the panel on every frame it plays, which replaces a pressed slider on the next frame (seen on the base in jsdom for this proposal), so a slider cannot be held beside a playing clock today either; no project or fixture carries a clock beside a ranged input, and this change leaves that case as it is and records it.
- The bank a drag leaves is the bank its sequence of requests leaves. For a travel in one direction it equals one request to the last position (checked for this proposal on 4,300 random splits, design Decision 3); a hand that crosses a commit's level back and forth fires it at each crossing the machine fires; a drag can be admitted step by step where one request to the same value would be refused whole, and can stop where that request would not. That is what the hand did, not a defect.
- Tests red on the base: a jsdom bench mounting the real viewer on hand-written clocked documents with a commit, a stop, a whole-number input, a scaled input, a refusal and a clock, dispatching a drag's events on the real slider and counting requests, poses, draws, panel replacements and outcome writes; a generated-sequence proof; mutations for every rule; browser tests in which Chromium's own mouse and touch drag a real slider and record each frame, and one that pins the browser's event order the design reads.
- The `on-demand` render mode does not change: it presents no clocked panel, so it has no slider. The posed and the running panels, the part controls, the mount handle's methods and their meaning, viewer API 30 and document versions 1 to 14 do not change.

## Capabilities

### New Capabilities

None.

### Modified Capabilities

- `viewer-package`: the requirement "A maker operates a clocked machine on screen" is restated: "every such gesture SHALL submit exactly ONE request" and "A gesture on a HANDLE SHALL be ONE request, and the transition that request reports SHALL BE DRAWN" now except a drag, which is defined with what it asks, when, and what it shows; the scenario "A slider's commit is drawn" now names a click on the track and the keyboard; new scenarios state the drag. The requirement "The viewer plays a clocked instruction as one drawn transition" is restated so that a dragged slider's followed positions are not among the gestures it draws, and its scenario "A handle's gesture is drawn like an instruction's press" excepts them. Design Decision 9 lists every sentence read and why the others hold.
