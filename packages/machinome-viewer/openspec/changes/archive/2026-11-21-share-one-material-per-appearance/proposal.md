## Why

On the Enigma I page (`enigma-i` at `081f4e6`, viewer at framework `e72d1ae7`) every one of the 3,347 meshes has a material object of its own, and they have 79 distinct appearances. three.js refreshes and uploads a material's uniforms whenever the material differs from the previous draw's, so a frame in which nothing moves pays for 3,346 changes of material. Pointing every mesh of one appearance at one material, from outside the viewer, took that frame from 22 to 30 ms of script to 17 to 21; with the shipped order of drawing kept it was 19 to 22 ms and no pixel of ten pictures differed, while letting three.js group the meshes by material changed one pixel in four of them (`workflow/warts.md`, "Every part has its own geometry and material, and an idle page draws all of it every frame"; `workflow/evidence/enigma-material-per-part-cost-2026-11-21.md`). The prototype exercised nothing of how a shared material is owned, changed or released; this change settles those before it takes the time, with the picture as the first requirement and the time as the second.

## What Changes

- `MaterialPresentation` (`presentation.ts`), today the one writer of a material's look after construction, becomes the holder of the viewer's materials: it makes every mesh a part, a flexible part or a marking draws with, and a mesh points at the one material of its appearance (the kind of material and its colour value) and of the look the overlays standing on it now call for (hovered, lit in a colour, dimmed, ghosted). The values are today's: the same tint, dimming factor, ghost opacity and hover grey, computed by the shipped formulas from the appearance's own material.
- No material is written after it is made. A hover, a highlight, a put-back and a colour the model chooses point meshes at other materials and back; a variant for a look is made on first use from the plain material of its appearance.
- The order the opaque parts are drawn in stays exactly today's. Today it is the order of the meshes' material numbers, which the tree reserves in document traversal order and renews where it replaces a mesh's material. Each mesh is given a place in that order at exactly those points, held on the mesh, and the viewer's renderer sorts the opaque list by it where three.js sorts by the material's number. The transparent list keeps three.js's own sort by depth.
- The holder counts the meshes of each appearance and of each look. A material no mesh points at is disposed, except the ghosted variants, which are kept while a mesh of their appearance remains so that ghosting again finds its shader compiled, as the shipped per-part materials do. Disposing a mesh releases its material instead of disposing it. One holder per viewer; the viewer's disposal leaves it empty.
- Tests that are red on the base by count: material objects among meshes of one appearance, changes of material along the drawn order, writes to a material after it was first handed to a mesh, and materials kept across a reload. A parity proof of the look against the shipped `MaterialPresentation`, kept inside the test as the oracle, over generated sequences, every material property compared exactly. The order of drawing and every mesh's look through a scripted sequence of the tree, recorded on the base and reproduced. Lifetime tests. A browser test with differently coloured coincident surfaces in an order grouping would change. Existing tests that assert one material per part are restated.
- Unchanged: geometry and its ownership, what is fetched and when, the lights, the renderer's settings, molejo, the mount handle's methods and their meaning, and every picture the viewer draws.

## Capabilities

### New Capabilities

None.

### Modified Capabilities

- `viewer-package`: "Fresh mounts draw loaded rigid assets independently of fetch completion order" states, in one sentence of its text and in three scenarios, that a failed or replaced load disposes "its material" and that with repeated paths "each part still owns separate geometry and material". Those sentences state the mechanism this change replaces. They are restated as what can be observed: nothing made for an abandoned mesh alone is retained, and disposing or replacing one part cannot change another's geometry or look. One scenario is added, for two parts of one colour. Every other word and scenario of the requirement is restated unchanged.
