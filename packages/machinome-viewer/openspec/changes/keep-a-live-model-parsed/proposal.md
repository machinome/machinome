## Why

On the Enigma I page (project `enigma-i` at `081f4e6`, a clocked document of version 14) a pressed button shows the instruction's start and its end and nothing between. The cause is the expression store's reclamation trigger: the mounted document holds 546,768 expression nodes, above `EXPRESSION_LIMITS.nodes` (125,000), so every outermost operation empties the store first and `bindings.ts`'s `roots()` prepares all 214,278 binding entries again, about 2.3 s each time. A press costs 4.6 to 7.2 s of script before its first frame and 1.7 to 3.6 s per frame after it. With the trigger moved out of the way in an otherwise unchanged build, the same presses cost 65 to 900 ms and the flap and hood drew 29 and 80 distinct pictures instead of 2 (`workflow/warts.md`, "A model larger than the store's ceiling is parsed again at every pose"; `workflow/evidence/enigma-press-script-cost-2026-11-20.md`).

This is the third time a supported document has met this line. `retain-large-expression-working-set` (22 September) raised it from 50,000 to 125,000 for the Curta's 102,690 nodes and wrote that "a later concrete project may justify a different policy"; `mount-voron-export` (5 October) met 483,728 nodes and declined to raise it again because doing so "would fit this printer by accident and conceal the same failure for the next larger supported document". The Enigma is the concrete project. This change replaces the size test between operations instead of moving the constant a third time.

## What Changes

- Ordinary operations (posing, drawing a frame, a clocked request, a run tick, a tree update) no longer reclaim the expression store, whatever its size. A mounted document's prepared graph is reused from one operation to the next; a successful load of a large document costs it no second preparation at its first operation.
- Reclamation at the finite headroom (`EXPRESSION_LIMITS.nodes`, unchanged at 125,000) happens where obsolete state is created: before the outermost synchronous validation of a document load (initial mount, reload, republish); at the end of a refused validation; when the viewer installs a validated replacement or abandons it after validation (a required asset that fails, a refusal for the render mode, a disposal during the load), the installation keeping the incoming graph; and at every viewer's disposal, the last one still emptying the store unconditionally. The design states and checks case by case the invariant this gives: at the start of every outermost operation that is not a load, fewer than 125,000 resident nodes belong to no loaded document, so retention stays bounded by the graphs of the loaded documents plus that headroom, never by publication history, refusals or disposals.
- The widget's own tests keep a way to reclaim before every outermost operation: a source-only switch, `EXPRESSION_LIMITS.everyOperation`, adds today's check at the start of every outermost operation to the reclamation points above. Every test that forces pressure sets it and asserts that a reclamation actually happened, so a policy change can no longer quietly empty those tests of meaning.
- New red-first tests at the default configuration: a synthetic document whose graph is larger than 125,000 nodes keeps its generation and its one binding preparation across adjacent operations; two loaded documents of very different size, using the same binding names for different expressions, are operated alternately with no reclamation between operations; repeated operation after load adds no expression nodes; loading and refusing replacements repeatedly keeps retention within a bound the design derives from the invariant. New tests of each reclamation point: a disposal that is not the last, a refused load, a replacement abandoned after validation, a standing graph rebuilt while a replacement loads, and an installation that keeps a large incoming graph; those that are green on the base are shown red against the implementation with their own reclamation removed.
- Evaluation, parsing, name resolution, generation guards, scope rules, refusals and numerical results are unchanged.

## Capabilities

### New Capabilities

None.

### Modified Capabilities

- `viewer-package`: "Expression cache pressure does not change a machine" replaces its fitting-graph promise (reuse across ticks only when the graph fits a finite capacity) with reuse across consecutive operations whatever the document's size, states where obsolete state is reclaimed (loads, installations, refusals at or after validation, and every viewer's disposal) and what bounds retention, and adds scenarios for a large document installed without a second preparation, alternating documents of different size, repeated operation adding no expression state, the disposal of a viewer that is not the last, and a refused replacement leaving no partial graph behind.

## Impact

- Source: `packages/machinome-viewer/machinome/viewer/web/widget/src/expressions.ts` (the reclamation decision in `withExpressions`, a load-scoped entry beside it, the settlement handle, the reclamation request of `releaseExpressions`, the test switch), `viewer.ts` (`assertRenderable` enters through the load-scoped entry and carries its settlement; `replaceTree` and `manifestChanged` settle each validated document as installed or abandoned), and the tests in `expression-lifetime.test.ts`, `validation-pressure.test.ts`, `expressions.test.ts` and `run/program.test.ts`, with new tests beside them. No other module's behaviour changes.
- Interfaces: viewer API stays 30 and the document versions read stay 1 to 14. `EXPRESSION_LIMITS`, `expressionMetrics`, the load-scoped entry and the settlement handle are widget-source exports and internal types; the bundle entry (`widget.ts`) exports none of them and nothing on the mount handle changes. No dependency, document schema, host option or published name changes.
- Memory: a large document's graph stays resident between operations instead of being emptied at the start of each one and rebuilt inside it. The base already holds a complete rebuilt store between operations, so this removes a full rebuild's garbage per operation rather than adding a resident structure of a new order; the design records what is retained and how it is measured.
- Records: a CHANGELOG bullet under "Unreleased" and, after implementation confirms the design, an amendment to ADR-043 (`workflow/adrs/EXPORT/`).
- What the originating project does with the result: the Enigma page is rebuilt against the new bundle and its presses are measured again by the evidence file's script-only method; the three other findings of the same warts section (flexible-part refills, the press path's double draw, the idle drawing load) remain open and are not addressed here.

Review, 20 November 2026. The repository agent reviewed the first draft against the source and against six recorded attack points, and accepted its policy: ordinary operations never reclaim by size, reclamation belongs where obsolete state is created, the constant stays as headroom. It confirmed that every document load enters through `loadDocument` and `assertRenderable`, and, on the Enigma page, that the store holds 546,768 nodes after load and the same number after nine instructions pressed. It returned two findings in which that draft made its own retention bound false: the disposal of a viewer that is not the last, and a refused load. A second agent revised the artifacts to close both, and found from the source two further cases the review had not named, a replacement refused after its validation and the standing graph rebuilt while a replacement loads; the settlement of a replacement answers them. The repository agent reviewed the revision, its invariant case by case and its planned tests, and approved proceeding to implementation. The cost it accepts is stated under the design’s Risks: in a page with more than one viewer, one viewer’s load, settlement or disposal can cost another viewer’s large graph one rebuild.
