1 · The lifecycle, and the three points the cycle attaches to

A frame is a class-body declaration and a mate is a class-body statement. Both are consumed before any render runs. The resolver is a compile step at realization, and its output is applied exactly where the author's own rest placement is captured.

2 · What one mate compiles into

The resolver produces nothing the framework does not already have. Thor's elbow is the instance: every number on the right is what the project writes by hand today.

3 · Which modules change

Two new modules, one signature change, and every arrow lands on a seam that already exists. The packages below the line are not touched by this cycle.

4 · Thor's elbow, today and after

The difference is the shape of the evidence. Today the elbow is two statements in two files that agree through three shared numbers. After, it is one statement per side, and the numbers are the design's own connectors, read from the same documents the layout is read from.

Two corrections to the working note

The spike answers back on two points of section 5. The x axis of a frame fixes a revolute mate's zero, not only a rigid mate's: with x left to its default the elbow's two lines still meet, but the forearm rests turned 120 degrees about the body diagonal. And the fixed end of a mate may be the declaring assembly's own frame, whose owner placement is the identity, which is the shape Thor's design already uses.
